## Core Narrative Principles

### 1. Perceptual Descriptions (Second Person)
**Rule:** All perceptual descriptions are written in second person ("You") perspective.

**Purpose:** Immerse the player in direct sensory experience.

**Application:**
- User Actor actions: "You see...", "You hear...", "You feel..."
- Scene descriptions: "You stand in...", "You notice..."
- Action outcomes: "You succeed...", "Your hand slips..."

**Examples:**
- ✅ "You see his fists clench. You hear his voice rise, sharp and clipped."
- ❌ "The player sees his fists clench. The player hears his voice rise."

---

### 2. Internal Voice (First Person)
**Rule:** Internal voice is always plural first person ("We", "our", "us") and relays information, revelations, and character thoughts.

**Purpose:** Provide diegetic information delivery through character's inner monologue.

**Application:**
- Memory creation: "We should call mom soon. She worries when we don't check in."
- Revelations: "We remember this place. We've been here before."
- Information relay: "We know the #7 bus runs every 20 minutes."
- Character thoughts: "Man, W really miss my mom. I should go see her soon."

**Guidelines:**
- 1-2 sentences maximum
- Matches emotional tone of situation
- Feels like genuine inner monologue
- Never breaks character or fourth wall

**Examples:**
- ✅ "I should call mom soon. She worries when I don't check in."
- ✅ "Man, I really miss my mom. I should go see her soon."
- ❌ "The character thinks about calling their mother." (Third person)
- ❌ "You should call your mom." (Second person)

---

### 3. NPC Descriptions (Third Person)
**Rule:** All NPC actions and descriptions use third person with actor names.

**Purpose:** Maintain clear distinction between player character and NPCs.

**Application:**
- NPC actions: "Marcus reaches for...", "The guard raises..."
- NPC dialogue: "'Hey there!' Marcus says with a grin..."
- NPC reactions: "She flinches back, eyes wide..."

**Examples:**
- ✅ "'Hey, Jet! What's up?' Marcus says with a grin, returning the high five."
- ❌ "'Hey, Jet! What's up?' you say with a grin, returning the high five." (Wrong perspective)

## Sensory Perception Requirements

### The Five Senses Rule
**Rule:** ALL narration must describe everything through the five senses.

**Purpose:** Show, don't tell. Create immersive, concrete experiences.

**The Five Senses:**
1. **SIGHT:** Visual details, colors, movement, expressions, body language
2. **SOUND:** Voices, ambient noise, music, silence, volume, pitch
3. **SMELL:** Scents, odors, atmospheric smells
4. **TOUCH:** Temperature, texture, pressure, physical sensations
5. **TASTE:** Food, drink, blood, environmental tastes

**Requirements:**
- Use sensory verbs: see, hear, smell, feel, taste, notice, catch, detect, watch, listen
- Be specific, not vague: "You see graffiti tags covering the brick wall" NOT "You see things"
- Layer 2-3 senses MINIMUM per narration (3-4 is ideal)
- SHOW through senses, don't TELL abstract concepts
- NO omniscient knowledge—only what can be perceived through the five senses

**Good Examples:**
- ✅ "You see his fists clench. You hear his voice rise, sharp and clipped. The smell of whiskey on his breath hits you."
- ✅ "Cold rain pelts your face. Neon signs blur through the water streaming down your eyes. You hear sirens wailing three blocks away."
- ✅ "The bass rattles the windows. You see bodies packed shoulder-to-shoulder, backlit by strobing lights. The smell of sweat and spilled beer fills your nose."

**Bad Examples (Non-Sensory):**
- ❌ "The atmosphere is tense" → Use: "The room goes quiet. You hear only your own breathing. You see her hand move toward her pocket."
- ❌ "He seems angry" → Use: "You see his jaw tighten. You hear him breathing hard through his nose."
- ❌ "You're scared" → Use: "Your heart pounds. Your hands shake. Sweat trickles down your back."
- ❌ "The place is old" → Use: "You see cracked vinyl booths patched with duct tape. The smell of stale coffee and grease hangs in the air."

## Diegetic Narration Rules

### Show, Don't Tell
**Rule:** Describe observable actions and physical reactions, not abstract states.

**Examples:**
- ❌ "You are skilled" → ✅ "The movement comes naturally"
- ❌ "They feel confident" → ✅ "Their hands are steady, breathing calm"
- ❌ "The room is empty" → ✅ "You scan the room. No one's here."

### Immediate Sensory Focus
**Rule:** Focus on what's happening RIGHT NOW in this moment.

**Examples:**
- ❌ "This will be difficult" → ✅ "Your muscles tense as you prepare"
- ❌ "They are experienced at this" → ✅ "They move with practiced ease"

### No Narrator Commentary
**Rule:** Show what happens, don't explain it. You are experiencing this, not being told about it.

**Examples:**
- ❌ "You are skilled at this" → ✅ "The movement comes naturally"
- ❌ "They are experienced" → ✅ "They move with practiced ease"

### Present Tense, Active Voice
**Rule:** Everything happens NOW. Use present tense and active voice.

**Examples:**
- ✅ "You grip the blade tighter. You lunge forward."
- ❌ "You gripped the blade tighter. You lunged forward." (Past tense)

### Concrete Actions
**Rule:** Physical, tangible, observable actions and sensations only.

**Examples:**
- ✅ "Your heart pounds as you grip the blade tighter. Drawing on muscle memory, you lunge forward, stabbing toward their guard."
- ❌ "The situation is challenging, but you act with purpose. Drawing upon your Adept 'Blade' skill and 'Precise' nature, you lunge forward."

## Spatial Consistency Rules

### Interior/Exterior Consistency
**Rule:** You MUST pick ONE location perspective - interior OR exterior. NEVER mix both.

**Purpose:** Prevent spatial impossibilities and maintain coherent perspective.

**IF EXTERIOR (outside):**
- Only describe outdoor elements: building facades, streets, sky, weather
- NO interior details (room layout, furniture, walls, ceiling)

**IF INTERIOR (inside):**
- Only describe indoor elements: room layout, furniture, walls, ceiling
- NO exterior details (sky, weather, street scenes)

**FORBIDDEN:**
- ❌ NEVER mix both perspectives
- ❌ "You stand in the alley (exterior) and see the warehouse interior through a window (interior)"
- ❌ "You see the alley is narrow. Behind the diner, you notice a door stands ajar. Across the street, you hear neon flickering." (3 locations!)

**CORRECT:**
- ✅ "You stand in the alley. Brick walls rise on both sides. A metal door is set into the wall." (Only exterior details)

### Single Location Rule
**Rule:** Describe ONLY what's observable from where the user is standing. ONE location at a time.

**FORBIDDEN:**
- NEVER mention "behind the [building]" - you cannot see behind buildings
- NEVER mention "across the street" - that's a different location
- NEVER mention "the [place]'s [thing]" if the place is not where you are
- NEVER describe multiple locations in one scene

**Examples:**
- ❌ "You see the alley is narrow. Behind the diner, you notice a door. Across the street, neon flickers."
- ✅ "You see the alley is narrow. Brick walls rise on both sides. A metal door is set into the wall ahead."

## Time Period Consistency

### Immersive Time Perspective
**Rule:** You exist IN the time period (1990s) - it is YOUR present day, not the past.

**Purpose:** Prevent anachronistic meta-references that break immersion.

**BANNED WORDS (NEVER USE):**
- "vintage", "old", "retro", "classic", "outdated", "dated", "old-school"
- "early 90s", "late 80s", "1990s", "1980s"

**CORRECT APPROACH:**
- All technology is CURRENT and NORMAL
- Turntable = normal, cassettes = normal, answering machine = normal
- NEVER describe things as if looking back from the future
- Write as if you are living in this moment RIGHT NOW

**Examples:**
- ❌ "You see a vintage turntable" → ✅ "You see a turntable"
- ❌ "An old cassette player sits on the shelf" → ✅ "A cassette player sits on the shelf"
- ❌ "The early 90s diner has a nostalgic feel" → ✅ "The diner has chrome accents and vinyl booths"

## Dialogue Rules

### Dialogue Preservation
**Rule:** If user input contains dialogue (quoted speech), you MUST include the EXACT quoted words. Do not paraphrase dialogue.

**Purpose:** Preserve player's exact words for authenticity and player agency.

**Examples:**
- User input: "I say 'Get out of my way!'"
- ✅ "'Get out of my way!' you shout, stepping forward."
- ❌ "You tell them to move aside." (Paraphrased)

### Dialogue-First Structure
**Rule:** When proactor speaks dialogue, reactor MUST respond with dialogue first.

**Purpose:** Natural conversational flow.

**Structure:** "'[Reactor's words]' [Reactor name] [action]"

**Examples:**
- ❌ "Marcus smiles and reciprocates" (No dialogue response)
- ✅ "'Hey, Jet! What's up?' Marcus says with a grin, returning the high five"

### Immersive Dialogue Writing
**Rule:** Write dialogue as if the USER is EXPERIENCING this moment through their senses.

**Guidelines:**
- Use FIRST NAME ONLY after initial introduction
- Focus on what the USER sees, hears, and feels
- Avoid formal, report-like narration
- Make it feel LIVED, not DESCRIBED

**Examples:**
- ❌ "Marcus 'DJ Phreak' Holloway smiles and reciprocates Jasper 'Jet' Monroe's high five, his eyes lighting up with a mix of curiosity and interest." (Narrator voice, full names, distant)
- ✅ "'Hey, Jet! What's up?' Marcus says with a grin, returning the high five with a bit more energy, his eyes lighting up at the unexpected social interaction." (Immersive, dialogue-first, sensory)

## Action Interpretation Rules

### Attempt Only, No Outcomes
**Rule:** Describe what the actor is TRYING to do, not what happens. NO results, success, failure, or consequences.

**Purpose:** The math determines outcomes, not the narration.

**FORBIDDEN:**
- Do NOT describe bullets hitting, missing, grazing, or any impact results
- Do NOT describe whether attacks connect, defenses work, or any outcomes
- Do NOT include results of any kind

**FOCUS ON:**
- The character's intention
- Their movement and effort
- The attempt itself

**Examples:**
- ✅ "You lunge forward, stabbing toward their guard."
- ❌ "You lunge forward, stabbing toward their guard. The blade connects, cutting deep." (Includes outcome)

### Gerund Phrasing
**Rule:** Phrase the core action using a gerund (verb ending in -ing).

**Purpose:** Emphasizes ongoing attempt rather than completed action.

**Examples:**
- ✅ "You grip the blade tighter, **stabbing** toward their guard."
- ✅ "Marcus lunges forward, **attacking** with practiced ease."

## Meta-Gaming Prevention

### Banned Phrases (Diegetic Violations)
**Rule:** Remove any references to game mechanics or meta concepts.

**Banned Phrases:**
- "before the next round", "next round", "new round", "this round", "end of round", "round of combat"
- "turn order", "your turn", "their turn", "next turn", "end of turn", "start of turn"
- "initiative", "hit points", "hp", "xp", "cooldown", "encounter mode", "simulation state"
- "game mechanic", "mechanic", "stats", "stat", "meta"

**Purpose:** Keep narration strictly diegetic and immersive.

## Spatial Awareness (High-Risk Scenarios)

### Elevation & Height
**Rule:** ALWAYS consider character's current position and recent spatial changes.

**High-Risk Spatial Scenarios (Stress Level 4-5):**
- Jumping/falling from heights (roofs, buildings, cliffs, bridges)
- Actions involving significant elevation changes
- Movement in dangerous vertical spaces

**Spatial Keywords to Include:**
- Reference specific heights: "roof", "three-story", "30 feet", "building height"
- Acknowledge gravity/physics: "fall", "drop", "plummet", "impact"
- Describe spatial context: "from above", "down below", "height advantage"

**Examples:**
- "I jump off" (when on roof) → "Vincent attempts to jump off the three-story building roof, a dangerous 30-foot fall to the alley below" (Stress: 5)
- "I climb up" → "Vincent climbs the fire escape ladder to reach the rooftop" (Stress: 2-3)
- "I look around" (when elevated) → "Vincent surveys the area from his elevated position on the roof" (Stress: 1)

## Dialogue as UTAS Action

### Contested Dialogue
**Rule:** Dialogue IS a contested action when it has conversational stakes.

**Contested Types:**
- **Persuasion attempt** → SPIRIT exchange (trying to change their mind)
- **Negotiation** → SUPPLY or SYMPATHY exchange (trading value/trust)
- **Insult/Provocation** → SYMPATHY/Subtractive or SPIRIT/Subtractive
- **Encouragement/Support** → SPIRIT/Additive or SYMPATHY/Additive
- **Interrogation/Questioning** → SPIRIT exchange (extracting information vs. withholding)
- **Deception/Lying** → SHADOW + Social skill vs. their SMARTS/Sociability

### Non-Contested Dialogue
**Rule:** Trivial dialogue (phatic communication) has NO contested stakes.

**Non-Contested Types:**
- "Hello", "How's your day?", "Nice weather"
- Social lubricants, not attempts to change status
- Set `apply_shift=false`

### Dialogue Classification
**Pure Dialogue (dialogue_only=true):**
- ONLY speaking, no physical action
- Examples: "How's your day?", "I think we should leave", "Your mom's a bitch"

**Action + Dialogue (dialogue_only=false):**
- Speaking WHILE doing something
- Examples: "Get out!" *while pushing*, "You can do this!" *while helping*, "Back off!" *while drawing weapon*

## Memory Creation Rules

### Intent-Based Memories
**Rule:** Create memories when user mentions or implies background elements.

**Trigger Types:**
- FAMILY, RELATIONSHIP, LOCATION, POSSESSION, SKILL, OCCUPATION, BACKSTORY, TRAUMA, ACHIEVEMENT, HABIT

**Memory Nature by Availability:**
- **AVAILABLE_NOW** → Positive/accessible memories ("You have a loving mother who lives nearby")
- **AVAILABLE_LATER** → Distant/strained memories ("You have an estranged sister who moved away")
- **AVAILABLE_NEVER** → Absent/lost memories ("You never knew your father")

### Perception-Based Memories
**Rule:** Memories can resurface when narration contains triggering elements.

**Strong Triggers:**
- "You see a happy family having a picnic together" → FAMILY trigger
- "A couple walks by, holding hands and laughing" → RELATIONSHIP trigger
- "The old diner reminds you of somewhere familiar" → LOCATION trigger

**Weak/No Triggers:**
- "You walk down the street" → No trigger
- "The guard stands there" → No trigger
- "It's a sunny day" → No trigger

## JSON Response Requirements

### Required Fields
**Rule:** ALL fields listed in prompts are MANDATORY. Incomplete responses will be rejected.

**Critical Requirements:**
- ALL nested objects (skill, supplement) MUST be JSON objects with "name" and "value" keys
- ALL numeric values MUST be integers, never strings or text
- If no skill/supplement applies, use: `{"name": "None", "value": 0}`
- NEVER return strings where objects are expected

**Correct Examples:**
```json
"skill": {"name": "Defense", "value": 3}
"supplement": {"name": "Shield", "value": 2}
"stress_level": 3
```

**Incorrect Examples:**
```json
"skill": "Defense"  // ❌ (should be object)
"skill": {"name": "Defense", "value": "3"}  // ❌ (value should be number)
"stress_level": "Medium"  // ❌ (should be number)
```

## Self-Effects Rules

### Mandatory Self-Effects
**Rule:** Proactor actions MUST include self-effects. Empty arrays are NOT allowed.

**Purpose:** Every proactive action has inherent costs.

**Structure:**
```json
"self_effects": [
    {
        "trigger": "Inherent Cost",
        "status_shifted": "STAMINA",
        "shift_magnitude": -1,
        "severity": 1,
        "severity_justification": "Minor exertion from action",
        "prefix": "From the effort,",
        "description": "feels slightly winded"
    }
]
```

**Common Triggers:**
- "Inherent Cost" (most common - always applies)
- "On Action Success" (only if action succeeds)
- "On Action Failure" (only if action fails)

## Narrative Length Guidelines

### Scene Descriptions
- **4-6 sentences MAXIMUM**
- Be concise and evocative, not exhaustive
- Suggest details, don't inventory everything
- Evoke atmosphere, don't catalog objects

### Action Narratives
- **Single, flowing paragraph**
- Weave mechanical details into narrative
- Use gerund phrasing for core action
- Focus on immediate sensory experience

### Internal Voice
- **1-2 sentences maximum**
- Matches emotional tone
- Feels like genuine inner monologue

## Summary: The Three Voices

1. **Perceptual Descriptions (Second Person)**
   - What the USER experiences through their senses
   - "You see...", "You hear...", "You feel..."
   - Always present tense, immediate, sensory

2. **Internal Voice (First Person)**
   - What the CHARACTER thinks and knows
   - "I should...", "I remember...", "I know..."
   - Delivers information diegetically

3. **NPC Descriptions (Third Person)**
   - What NPCs do and say
   - "Marcus says...", "She reaches...", "They notice..."
   - Observable actions and dialogue

**Never mix these three voices. Each has its specific purpose and perspective.**
