# Sensory Narration Standard - Five Senses Perception

## Philosophy

ALL narration should be described through the **five senses** (sight, sound, smell, touch, taste). This creates immersive, grounded storytelling that shows rather than tells.

---

## The Five Senses

### **1. SIGHT (Visual)**
- What the character sees
- Colors, shapes, movement
- Light, shadow, darkness
- Facial expressions, body language

**Examples:**
- ✅ "You see the bartender's eyes narrow as he wipes down the counter."
- ✅ "Neon signs flicker in the rain-slicked street outside."
- ❌ "The bartender is suspicious." (tells, doesn't show)

### **2. SOUND (Auditory)**
- What the character hears
- Voices, music, ambient noise
- Volume, pitch, rhythm
- Silence and its quality

**Examples:**
- ✅ "You hear the jukebox playing a muffled bass line through the wall."
- ✅ "Her voice drops to a whisper."
- ❌ "There's music playing." (vague, not sensory)

### **3. SMELL (Olfactory)**
- What the character smells
- Strong or subtle scents
- Pleasant or unpleasant odors
- Atmospheric smells

**Examples:**
- ✅ "The smell of cigarette smoke and stale beer hangs in the air."
- ✅ "You catch a whiff of gasoline and rust."
- ❌ "The place smells bad." (tells, not specific)

### **4. TOUCH (Tactile)**
- What the character feels physically
- Temperature, texture, pressure
- Pain, comfort, discomfort
- Physical sensations

**Examples:**
- ✅ "The cold metal of the door handle bites into your palm."
- ✅ "Sweat trickles down your back."
- ❌ "It's cold." (tells, not sensory)

### **5. TASTE (Gustatory)**
- What the character tastes
- Food, drink, ambient tastes
- Metallic, sweet, bitter, etc.
- Mouth sensations

**Examples:**
- ✅ "The bitter taste of cheap coffee lingers on your tongue."
- ✅ "You taste copper—blood from your split lip."
- ❌ "The coffee is bad." (tells, not sensory)

---

## Sensory Language Guidelines

### **Use Sensory Verbs:**

**Sight:** see, watch, notice, glimpse, spot, observe, look
**Sound:** hear, listen, catch (a sound), pick up
**Smell:** smell, catch a whiff, detect, breathe in
**Touch:** feel, touch, sense, grip, brush against
**Taste:** taste, savor, detect

### **Be Specific:**

❌ **Vague:** "You notice something."
✅ **Specific:** "You see a shadow move across the alley entrance."

❌ **Vague:** "It sounds loud."
✅ **Specific:** "The bass rattles the windows, drowning out conversation."

❌ **Vague:** "It smells."
✅ **Specific:** "The acrid smell of burning rubber drifts through the open window."

### **Layer Multiple Senses:**

Good narration often combines 2-3 senses:

✅ "You see the bartender's jaw tighten as you hear the crack of his knuckles. The smell of stale beer and cigarettes thickens the air between you."

✅ "The cold rain pelts your face as you hear sirens wailing in the distance. Neon signs blur through the water streaming down your eyes."

---

## What to AVOID

### **❌ Abstract Descriptions:**
- "The atmosphere is tense." → ✅ "You feel your shoulders tighten. The room has gone quiet except for the hum of the refrigerator."
- "He seems angry." → ✅ "You see his fists clench. His voice rises, sharp and clipped."

### **❌ Telling Instead of Showing:**
- "You're scared." → ✅ "Your heart pounds. Your hands shake as you grip the door handle."
- "It's dangerous." → ✅ "You hear glass crunch under your feet. Shadows move in the broken windows above."

### **❌ Omniscient Knowledge:**
- "He's thinking about his past." → ✅ "You see his eyes go distant, unfocused."
- "She's planning something." → ✅ "You notice her glance toward the exit, then back to you."

### **❌ Generic Sensory Details:**
- "You see things." → ✅ "You see graffiti tags covering the brick wall, a broken bottle glinting in the gutter."
- "You hear sounds." → ✅ "You hear a car alarm wailing three blocks away, footsteps echoing in the alley."

## Application by Narration Type

### **Scene Descriptions**

**Focus:** Visual + Auditory + Olfactory

```
✅ GOOD:
You see the diner's neon sign flickering red and blue through the rain-streaked window. 
Inside, you hear the hiss of the grill and the clink of coffee cups. The smell of frying 
bacon and burnt coffee fills the air. Vinyl booths line the walls, their red surfaces 
cracked and patched with duct tape.

❌ BAD:
The diner is old and run-down. It's a typical greasy spoon. The atmosphere is nostalgic.
```

### **Action Results**

**Focus:** All senses relevant to the action

```
✅ GOOD:
You swing the wrench. It connects with a hollow clang that reverberates up your arm. 
The pipe shudders, and you hear water hissing as pressure releases. Cold spray hits 
your face, tasting faintly metallic.

❌ BAD:
You hit the pipe with the wrench. It works. Water comes out.
```

### **NPC Interactions**

**Focus:** Visual (body language) + Auditory (voice, tone)

```
✅ GOOD:
The bartender's eyes narrow. You hear his voice drop to a growl. "You got a problem?" 
His knuckles whiten as he grips the bar towel. You smell whiskey on his breath.

❌ BAD:
The bartender is angry and confrontational. He doesn't like you.
```

### **Exploration Opportunities**

**Focus:** Sensory hooks that invite investigation

```
✅ GOOD:
You notice a door slightly ajar at the end of the hallway. You hear muffled voices 
from inside. A sliver of yellow light spills across the floor. You smell cigarette 
smoke drifting out.

❌ BAD:
There's a door you could investigate. It might be interesting.
```

## Sensory Perception Checklist

For every narration, ask:

- [ ] **What does the character SEE?** (Visual details)
- [ ] **What does the character HEAR?** (Sounds, voices, ambient noise)
- [ ] **What does the character SMELL?** (Scents, odors, atmosphere)
- [ ] **What does the character FEEL?** (Physical sensations, temperature, texture)
- [ ] **What does the character TASTE?** (If relevant to the action)

**Minimum:** Every narration should include at least 2-3 senses.

**Ideal:** Layer 3-4 senses for rich, immersive description.

## Examples by Scene Type

### **Urban Street Scene**

```
✅ SENSORY-RICH:
You step onto the sidewalk. Neon signs reflect in puddles at your feet—red, blue, 
flickering green. You hear traffic rumbling past, a car horn blaring two blocks away. 
The smell of exhaust and wet asphalt fills your nose. Cold rain soaks through your 
jacket, and you feel it running down your neck.

❌ NON-SENSORY:
You're on the street. It's raining and there are cars. The city is busy.
```

### **Bar/Club Interior**

```
✅ SENSORY-RICH:
You push through the door. Bass throbs through the floor, rattling your chest. You 
see bodies packed shoulder-to-shoulder, backlit by strobing lights. The smell of 
sweat, spilled beer, and cigarette smoke hits you. Your shoes stick to the floor 
with each step.

❌ NON-SENSORY:
You enter the club. It's crowded and loud. People are dancing.
```

### **Combat/Conflict**

```
✅ SENSORY-RICH:
His fist connects with your jaw. You taste copper as your teeth cut the inside of 
your cheek. Your vision blurs. You hear him breathing hard, smell his sweat. Your 
knees buckle, and you feel the rough concrete scrape your palms as you catch yourself.

❌ NON-SENSORY:
He punches you. It hurts. You fall down but catch yourself.
```

### **Quiet/Tense Moment**

```
✅ SENSORY-RICH:
The room goes silent. You hear only the hum of the refrigerator and your own breathing. 
You see her hand move slowly toward her pocket. Your pulse pounds in your ears. The 
air feels thick, heavy.

❌ NON-SENSORY:
It's quiet and tense. She might be reaching for a weapon. You're nervous.
```

## Summary

**Core Principle:** If it can't be perceived through the five senses, don't narrate it.

**Always Ask:** What would the character actually SEE, HEAR, SMELL, FEEL, and TASTE in this moment?

**Result:** Immersive, grounded, "show don't tell" narration that puts the player IN the scene, not observing it from outside.
