# Realitas Narrative Output Validation Checklist

**Purpose:** This document is the definitive checklist for validating ALL narrative outputs from the Realitas simulation. When reviewing simulation output, check each applicable section against the rules below. Mark items as ✅ PASS or ❌ FAIL.

**How to Use:** Copy-paste simulation output into your review tool, then systematically verify each section that applies to the output type you're reviewing.

## A. PERSPECTIVE RULES

### A1. User Actor (UA) Descriptions - SECOND PERSON
**Rule:** ALL perceptual descriptions involving the UA use second person ("You").

**CORRECT Examples:**
- ✅ "You see his fists clench."
- ✅ "You hear the door creak open."
- ✅ "You feel the cold metal against your palm."
- ✅ "You notice a faint smell of smoke."

**INCORRECT Examples:**
- ❌ "The player sees his fists clench." (third person reference to player)
- ❌ "Alex sees his fists clench." (using UA's name instead of "you")
- ❌ "One can see his fists clench." (impersonal)

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] Does every UA perception use "You see/hear/feel/smell/taste/notice"?
- [ ] Is the UA never referred to by name in perceptual descriptions?
- [ ] Is the UA never referred to in third person?

---

### A2. Non-User Actor (NUA) Descriptions - THIRD PERSON
**Rule:** ALL NUA actions and descriptions use third person with actor names.

**CORRECT Examples:**
- ✅ "Marcus reaches for the door handle."
- ✅ "'Hey there!' Sarah says with a grin."
- ✅ "The guard raises his weapon."
- ✅ "She flinches back, eyes wide."

**INCORRECT Examples:**
- ❌ "You reach for the door handle." (second person for NUA)
- ❌ "I reach for the door handle." (first person for NUA)

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] Are all NUA actions described in third person?
- [ ] Are NUA names used (not "you" or "I")?
- [ ] After initial introduction, are FIRST NAMES ONLY used?

---

### A3. Internal Voice - FIRST PERSON SINGULAR
**Rule:** Internal voice uses first person singular ("I", "my", "me") and represents the UA's inner thoughts.

**CORRECT Examples:**
- ✅ "I should call mom soon. She worries when I don't check in."
- ✅ "I remember this place. I've been here before."
- ✅ "Man, I really miss my mom."

**INCORRECT Examples:**
- ❌ "We should call mom soon." (plural first person)
- ❌ "You should call your mom." (second person)
- ❌ "The character thinks about calling their mother." (third person)

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] Does internal voice use "I/my/me" (first person singular)?
- [ ] Is it 1-2 sentences maximum?
- [ ] Does it feel like genuine inner monologue?
- [ ] Does it match the emotional tone of the situation?

---

### A4. No Perspective Mixing
**Rule:** Each voice type stays in its lane. Never mix perspectives within a single output type.

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] Are perceptual descriptions consistently second person?
- [ ] Are NUA descriptions consistently third person?
- [ ] Is internal voice consistently first person singular?
- [ ] Is there NO mixing of perspectives within a single narrative block?

## B. SENSORY IMMERSION RULES

### B1. The Five Senses Rule
**Rule:** ALL narration must describe everything through the five senses. Use 2-3+ senses MINIMUM per narration (3-4 is ideal).

**The Five Senses:**
1. **SIGHT:** Visual details, colors, movement, expressions, body language
2. **SOUND:** Voices, ambient noise, music, silence, volume, pitch
3. **SMELL:** Scents, odors, atmospheric smells
4. **TOUCH:** Temperature, texture, pressure, physical sensations
5. **TASTE:** Food, drink, blood, environmental tastes

**CORRECT Examples:**
- ✅ "You see his fists clench. You hear his voice rise, sharp and clipped. The smell of whiskey on his breath hits you." (3 senses: sight, sound, smell)
- ✅ "Cold rain pelts your face. Neon signs blur through the water streaming down your eyes. You hear sirens wailing three blocks away." (3 senses: touch, sight, sound)
- ✅ "The bass rattles the windows. You see bodies packed shoulder-to-shoulder, backlit by strobing lights. The smell of sweat and spilled beer fills your nose." (3 senses: sound, sight, smell)

**INCORRECT Examples:**
- ❌ "The atmosphere is tense." (abstract, no senses)
- ❌ "He seems angry." (interpretation, not sensory)
- ❌ "You're scared." (telling emotion, not showing through senses)
- ❌ "The place is old." (abstract description)

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] Does the narration use at least 2-3 different senses?
- [ ] Are sensory verbs used (see, hear, smell, feel, taste, notice, catch, detect)?
- [ ] Are descriptions concrete and specific, not vague?
- [ ] Is there NO abstract state description (tense, angry, scared, old)?

---

### B2. Sensory Verb Sentence Structure
**Rule:** Sensory perception verbs must appear at the START of sentences, not buried mid-sentence.

**CORRECT Examples:**
- ✅ "You glimpse a stack of dusty ledgers as she lifts the shutter."
- ✅ "You notice his hand trembling slightly."
- ✅ "You catch the faint scent of perfume."

**INCORRECT Examples:**
- ❌ "As she lifts the shutter, you glimpse a stack of dusty ledgers." (perception verb buried)
- ❌ "When he speaks, you notice his hand trembling." (perception verb buried)

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] Do sentences with perception verbs START with "You [perception verb]..."?
- [ ] Are perception verbs NOT buried after subordinate clauses?

---

### B3. Show, Don't Tell
**Rule:** Describe observable actions and physical reactions, not abstract states.

**CORRECT Examples:**
- ✅ "The movement comes naturally." (NOT "You are skilled")
- ✅ "Their hands are steady, breathing calm." (NOT "They feel confident")
- ✅ "You scan the room. No one's here." (NOT "The room is empty")

**INCORRECT Examples:**
- ❌ "You are skilled at this."
- ❌ "They feel confident."
- ❌ "This will be difficult."
- ❌ "They are experienced at this."

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] Are there NO abstract state descriptions?
- [ ] Are emotions shown through physical signs, not stated directly?
- [ ] Are skills/abilities shown through actions, not declared?

---

### B4. No Omniscient Knowledge
**Rule:** Only describe what can be perceived through the five senses. NO mind-reading, NO knowledge of things outside perception range.

**CORRECT Examples:**
- ✅ "You see his jaw tighten." (observable)
- ✅ "You hear frustration in her voice." (audible)

**INCORRECT Examples:**
- ❌ "He is thinking about his past." (mind-reading)
- ❌ "She feels betrayed." (internal state not observable)
- ❌ "Meanwhile, across town, the villain plots..." (outside perception range)

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] Is everything described actually perceivable by the UA?
- [ ] Are there NO descriptions of NUA internal thoughts/feelings?
- [ ] Is there NO information from outside the UA's perception range?

## C. SPATIAL CONSISTENCY RULES

### C1. Interior/Exterior Consistency
**Rule:** Pick ONE location perspective - interior OR exterior. NEVER mix both.

**IF EXTERIOR (outside):**
- Only describe outdoor elements: building facades, streets, sky, weather
- NO interior details (room layout, furniture, walls, ceiling)

**IF INTERIOR (inside):**
- Only describe indoor elements: room layout, furniture, walls, ceiling
- NO exterior details (sky, weather, street scenes)

**INCORRECT Examples:**
- ❌ "You stand in the alley (exterior) and see the warehouse interior through a window (interior)."
- ❌ "You see the alley is narrow. Behind the diner, you notice a door stands ajar. Across the street, you hear neon flickering." (3 locations!)

**CORRECT Examples:**
- ✅ "You stand in the alley. Brick walls rise on both sides. A metal door is set into the wall." (Only exterior details)

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] Is the perspective consistently interior OR exterior?
- [ ] Are there NO mixed interior/exterior descriptions?
- [ ] Is only ONE location described at a time?

---

### C2. Single Location Rule
**Rule:** Describe ONLY what's observable from where the UA is standing. ONE location at a time.

**FORBIDDEN Phrases:**
- ❌ "Behind the [building]" - you cannot see behind buildings
- ❌ "Across the street" - that's a different location
- ❌ "The [place]'s [thing]" if the place is not where you are
- ❌ Multiple locations in one scene

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] Is only ONE location described?
- [ ] Can everything described be seen from the UA's current position?
- [ ] Are there NO references to "behind," "across," or other locations?

---

### C3. Elevation Awareness
**Rule:** ALWAYS consider character's current position and recent spatial changes. High-risk spatial scenarios require explicit acknowledgment.

**High-Risk Spatial Scenarios (Stress Level 4-5):**
- Jumping/falling from heights (roofs, buildings, cliffs, bridges)
- Actions involving significant elevation changes
- Movement in dangerous vertical spaces

**Required Spatial Keywords:**
- Reference specific heights: "roof", "three-story", "30 feet", "building height"
- Acknowledge gravity/physics: "fall", "drop", "plummet", "impact"
- Describe spatial context: "from above", "down below", "height advantage"

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] If elevated, is the height acknowledged?
- [ ] If jumping/falling, are spatial consequences mentioned?
- [ ] Are elevation changes treated with appropriate stress levels?

## D. TIME PERIOD CONSISTENCY

### D1. Immersive Time Perspective
**Rule:** You exist IN the time period - it is YOUR present day, not the past.

**BANNED WORDS (NEVER USE):**
- ❌ "vintage"
- ❌ "old"
- ❌ "retro"
- ❌ "classic"
- ❌ "outdated"
- ❌ "dated"
- ❌ "old-school"
- ❌ "early 90s", "late 80s", "1990s", "1980s" (or any era reference)

**CORRECT Approach:**
- All technology is CURRENT and NORMAL
- Turntable = normal, cassettes = normal, answering machine = normal
- NEVER describe things as if looking back from the future
- Write as if you are living in this moment RIGHT NOW

**CORRECT Examples:**
- ✅ "You see a turntable." (NOT "vintage turntable")
- ✅ "A cassette player sits on the shelf." (NOT "old cassette player")
- ✅ "The diner has chrome accents and vinyl booths." (NOT "early 90s diner with nostalgic feel")

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] Are there NO banned anachronistic words?
- [ ] Is technology described as normal/current, not old/vintage?
- [ ] Is there NO meta-reference to the time period?

---

### D2. Setting-Appropriate Content
**Rule:** All content must match the worldbuilding setting. No anachronisms.

**Examples of Setting Violations:**
- Medieval setting: NO phones, cars, computers, modern slang
- 1990s setting: NO smartphones, internet references, modern apps
- Futuristic setting: Technology should match the era

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] Does all technology match the setting's time period?
- [ ] Does language/slang match the setting?
- [ ] Are there NO anachronistic references?

## E. DIEGETIC NARRATION RULES

### E1. Present Tense, Active Voice
**Rule:** Everything happens NOW. Use present tense and active voice.

**CORRECT Examples:**
- ✅ "You grip the blade tighter. You lunge forward."
- ✅ "Marcus opens the door."

**INCORRECT Examples:**
- ❌ "You gripped the blade tighter. You lunged forward." (past tense)
- ❌ "The door was opened by Marcus." (passive voice)

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] Is all narration in present tense?
- [ ] Is active voice used (subject performs action)?

---

### E2. No Meta-Gaming Language
**Rule:** Remove any references to game mechanics or meta concepts.

**BANNED PHRASES (NEVER USE):**
- ❌ "before the next round", "next round", "new round", "this round", "end of round", "round of combat"
- ❌ "turn order", "your turn", "their turn", "next turn", "end of turn", "start of turn"
- ❌ "initiative", "hit points", "hp", "xp", "cooldown", "encounter mode", "simulation state"
- ❌ "game mechanic", "mechanic", "stats", "stat", "meta"
- ❌ "stress level", "success roll", "serendipity roll"

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] Are there NO game mechanic references?
- [ ] Are there NO meta-gaming terms?
- [ ] Does everything stay in-world and immersive?

---

### E3. No Motivation Explanations
**Rule:** Scene descriptions must NEVER explain character motivations or reference personality traits explicitly.

**FORBIDDEN PATTERNS:**
- ❌ "Your hands reach for the notebook, a habit born from your imposter syndrome."
- ❌ "You glance nervously around, reflecting your anxiety."
- ❌ "You move cautiously due to your paranoia."
- ❌ "A gesture showing your confidence."

**CORRECT Approach:**
- ✅ "Your hands reach for the nearby notebook."
- ✅ "You glance around."
- ✅ "You move cautiously."
- Show behavior, don't explain it.

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] Are there NO explanations of WHY the character does things?
- [ ] Are there NO references to personality traits in narration?
- [ ] Is behavior shown without psychological explanation?

## F. ACTION INTERPRETATION RULES

### F1. Attempt Only, No Outcomes
**Rule:** Describe what the actor is TRYING to do, not what happens. NO results, success, failure, or consequences.

**FORBIDDEN:**
- Do NOT describe bullets hitting, missing, grazing, or any impact results
- Do NOT describe whether attacks connect, defenses work, or any outcomes
- Do NOT include results of any kind

**FOCUS ON:**
- The character's intention
- Their movement and effort
- The attempt itself

**CORRECT Examples:**
- ✅ "You lunge forward, stabbing toward their guard."
- ✅ "Marcus swings the bat toward your head."

**INCORRECT Examples:**
- ❌ "You lunge forward, stabbing toward their guard. The blade connects, cutting deep." (includes outcome)
- ❌ "Marcus swings the bat and misses." (includes outcome)

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] Does the narration describe only the ATTEMPT?
- [ ] Are there NO results (hit, miss, success, failure)?
- [ ] Is the outcome left undetermined?

---

### F2. Gerund Phrasing
**Rule:** Phrase the core action using a gerund (verb ending in -ing).

**CORRECT Examples:**
- ✅ "You grip the blade tighter, **stabbing** toward their guard."
- ✅ "Marcus lunges forward, **attacking** with practiced ease."
- ✅ "She steps back, **dodging** the incoming blow."

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] Is the core action described with a gerund (-ing verb)?
- [ ] Does the gerund emphasize ongoing attempt rather than completed action?

---

## G. INTERNAL VOICE RULES

### G1. Format Requirements
**Rule:** Internal voice has strict format requirements.

**Requirements:**
- First person singular ("I", "my", "me")
- 1-2 sentences MAXIMUM
- Matches emotional tone of situation
- Feels like genuine inner monologue
- Never breaks character or fourth wall
- NO explicit sensory verbs ("I see", "I hear") - state thoughts directly

**CORRECT Examples:**
- ✅ "I should call mom soon. She worries when I don't check in."
- ✅ "Notebooks like this - always a mix of random thoughts and important notes."
- ✅ "Receiver's off the hook? Who left it like that?"

**INCORRECT Examples:**
- ❌ "We've seen notebooks like this before." (explicit sensory verb, plural)
- ❌ "I can see that the phone is off the hook." (explicit sensory verb)
- ❌ Long paragraph of internal monologue (too long)

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] Is it first person singular (I/my/me)?
- [ ] Is it 1-2 sentences maximum?
- [ ] Are there NO explicit sensory verbs (I see, I hear, I notice)?
- [ ] Does it feel like natural inner thought?
- [ ] Does it match the emotional context?

---

### G2. Content Engagement
**Rule:** Internal voice must engage with SPECIFIC DETAILS from what was just perceived, not be generic.

**CORRECT Examples:**
- ✅ "Receiver's off the hook? Who left it like that? And that dial tone - someone trying to reach us?" (engages with specific details)

**INCORRECT Examples:**
- ❌ "We've seen phones like this before. This one seems a bit off though." (vague, generic)

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] Does the internal voice reference specific details from the scene?
- [ ] Is it NOT generic or vague?
- [ ] Does it engage with story hooks or interesting elements?

## H. EXCHANGE SYSTEM RULES

### H1. Exchange Structure
**Rule:** Contested actions follow a specific 6-step structure with 3 user-facing outputs per round.

**User-Facing Outputs per Round:**
1. **Step 2:** Proactor action description
2. **Step 4:** Reactor action description
3. **Step 6:** Final outcome description

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] Does each exchange round have exactly 3 narrative outputs?
- [ ] Is Step 2 the proactor's attempt (no outcome)?
- [ ] Is Step 4 the reactor's attempt (no outcome)?
- [ ] Is Step 6 the outcome and status shift?

---

### H2. Self-Effects (Proactor Only)
**Rule:** Every proactor action MUST include self-effects. Empty arrays are NOT allowed.

**Required Self-Effect Structure:**
- **Trigger:** "Inherent Cost", "On Action Success", or "On Action Failure"
- **Target Status:** STAMINA, SPIRIT, or SUPPLY
- **Polarity:** Additive or Subtractive
- **Shift Type:** Lasting or Temporary
- **Severity:** 1-4 (4 only for exceptional cases)

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] Does every proactor action have at least one self-effect?
- [ ] Is the self-effect array NOT empty?
- [ ] Does each self-effect have all required fields?
- [ ] Is severity between 1-4?

---

### H3. Secondary Effects (Reactor Only)
**Rule:** Reactors may have secondary effects (reactive strike or boon), but NOT self-effects.

**Secondary Effect Types:**
- **Reactive Strike:** Targets the Proactor
- **Reactive Boon:** Targets Self (the Reactor)

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] Does the reactor have NO self-effects?
- [ ] If secondary effect exists, is target correctly specified (Proactor or Self)?
- [ ] Is secondary effect properly justified?

---

## I. STEP 6 OUTCOME NARRATIVE RULES

### I1. Content Focus
**Rule:** Step 6 focuses ONLY on the outcome (who won/lost) and the resulting status shift.

**What Step 6 Covers:**
- Who won the exchange (proactor vs reactor)
- The resulting status shift (boost or penalty)
- WHY the status changed based on the outcome's emotional/physical impact

**What Step 6 Does NOT Cover:**
- Proactor self-effects/costs (those are separate)
- Both actors' changes (only the affected actor from the outcome)
- Dialogue repetition from Steps 2 and 4

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] Does Step 6 clearly state who won?
- [ ] Does it describe the status shift to the affected actor?
- [ ] Is it 2-3 sentences MAXIMUM?
- [ ] Does it NOT repeat dialogue from earlier steps?
- [ ] Does it NOT describe self-effects (those are separate)?

---

### I2. N2N Descriptor Format
**Rule:** Step 6 must use Numerical-to-Narrative (N2N) descriptors for status shifts.

**N2N Descriptor Scale:**
| Value | Descriptor |
|-------|------------|
| -5 | Superb Reverse |
| -4 | Extraordinary Reverse |
| -3 | Average Reverse |
| -2 | Subpar Reverse |
| -1 | Minimal Reverse |
| 0 | Null |
| 1 | Minimal |
| 2 | Subpar |
| 3 | Average |
| 4 | Extraordinary |
| 5 | Superb |

**Example Output:**
"You hear genuine excitement in Marcus's voice as he responds to your idea. His enthusiasm lifts your confidence, you experience a MINIMAL BOOST to your SPIRIT."

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] Does the outcome use N2N descriptors (Minimal, Subpar, Average, etc.)?
- [ ] Is the status type named (STAMINA, SPIRIT, SUPPLY)?
- [ ] Is the shift direction clear (BOOST or PENALTY)?

## J. DIALOGUE RULES

### J1. Dialogue Preservation
**Rule:** If user input contains dialogue (quoted speech), include the EXACT quoted words. Do not paraphrase.

**CORRECT Examples:**
- User input: "I say 'Get out of my way!'"
- ✅ "'Get out of my way!' you shout, stepping forward."
- ❌ "You tell them to move aside." (Paraphrased)

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] Are user's quoted words preserved EXACTLY?
- [ ] Is there NO paraphrasing of user dialogue?

---

### J2. Unquoted Dialogue Handling
**Rule:** Unquoted dialogue (ask, tell, greet) IS dialogue but preserve the user's phrasing. NEVER invent specific quoted words.

**CORRECT Examples:**
- Input: "I ask what they're doing"
- ✅ "You ask what they're doing."
- ❌ "You say 'What are you doing here?'" (invented quote)

- Input: "I greet them"
- ✅ "You greet them warmly."
- ❌ "You say 'Hello there!'" (invented quote)

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] For unquoted dialogue, is the user's phrasing preserved?
- [ ] Are there NO invented specific quoted words?
- [ ] Are sensory details (tone, body language) added without inventing dialogue content?

---

### J3. Dialogue-First Structure
**Rule:** When proactor speaks dialogue, reactor MUST respond with dialogue first.

**Structure:** "'[Reactor's words]' [Reactor name] [action]"

**CORRECT Examples:**
- ✅ "'Hey, Jet! What's up?' Marcus says with a grin, returning the high five."

**INCORRECT Examples:**
- ❌ "Marcus smiles and reciprocates." (No dialogue response)

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] When proactor speaks, does reactor respond with dialogue FIRST?
- [ ] Is dialogue followed by action description?

---

### J4. Contested vs Non-Contested Dialogue
**Rule:** Dialogue with stakes is contested; trivial dialogue is not.

**Contested Dialogue (has stakes):**
- Persuasion attempt → SPIRIT exchange
- Negotiation → SUPPLY or SYMPATHY exchange
- Insult/Provocation → SYMPATHY/Subtractive or SPIRIT/Subtractive
- Encouragement/Support → SPIRIT/Additive or SYMPATHY/Additive
- Interrogation/Questioning → SPIRIT exchange
- Deception/Lying → SHADOW + Social skill vs. SMARTS/Sociability

**Non-Contested Dialogue (no stakes):**
- "Hello", "How's your day?", "Nice weather"
- Social lubricants, not attempts to change status

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] Is dialogue with stakes treated as contested?
- [ ] Is trivial dialogue treated as non-contested?
- [ ] Is the exchange type appropriate for the dialogue intent?

## K. INQUIRY SYSTEM RULES

### K1. Sensory Perception Limit
**Rule:** Inquiries can only be answered if the information is perceivable through the five senses.

**ALLOWED Inquiries:**
- ✅ "How many people are here?" (countable by sight)
- ✅ "What weapons can I see?" (visible)
- ✅ "Can I hear footsteps?" (audible)

**BLOCKED Inquiries:**
- ❌ "Why is he trying to hurt me?" (requires mind-reading)
- ❌ "What is she thinking?" (internal state)
- ❌ "What happened here before I arrived?" (outside perception)

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] Can the inquiry be answered through the five senses?
- [ ] If blocked, is there an immersive narrative explanation?

---

### K2. Written Material Content
**Rule:** When UA reads written materials (notebooks, books, flyers, signs, letters), the ACTUAL TEXT CONTENT must be included.

**CORRECT Examples:**
- ✅ "You see a notebook entry: 'Day 47 - Need to check the back door before tonight's meeting.'"
- ✅ "You see a bright red flyer: 'UNDERGROUND RAVE - Tonight at midnight - Warehouse District'"

**INCORRECT Examples:**
- ❌ "You see handwritten text in the notebook." (Doesn't show what it says!)
- ❌ "You see a flyer about a rave." (Doesn't show the actual text!)

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] When reading written materials, is the actual text content shown?
- [ ] Is the text presented as direct visual perception?

- [ ] Are mechanical details woven in, not listed separately?

### L3. Internal Voice

**Validation Questions:**

## M. PHONE CALL RULES

### M1. Remote Encounter Constraints
**Rule:** Phone calls MUST remain phone conversations throughout. The system must NOT teleport NPCs into physical presence.

**ALLOWED Actions (Phone Call):**
- Speaking, listening
- Asking questions, sharing information
- Making plans, ending the call
- Format: `says "[dialogue]" in a [tone] voice`

**FORBIDDEN Actions (Phone Call):**
- ❌ "approaches", "walks to", "touches", "hands over"
- ❌ Any physical proximity actions
- ❌ Any action requiring physical presence

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] Are all phone call actions limited to verbal communication?
- [ ] Are there NO physical actions during phone calls?
- [ ] Does the NUA NOT suddenly appear in physical presence?

---

### M2. Phone Call Format
**Rule:** Phone call dialogue uses specific format.

**Phone Call Format:**
- `[Name] says "[words]" in a [tone] voice`

**In-Person Format (for comparison):**
- `[Name] says "[words]" while [physical action]`

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] Does phone dialogue use the correct format?
- [ ] Is there NO "while [physical action]" during phone calls?

## N. CONTINUITY RULES

### N1. NPC Manifestation Prevention
**Rule:** NPCs cannot be manifested from user intent alone. All NPC interactions must respect continuity.

**NPCs Can Only Exist Through:**
1. Scene Description - Auto-spawned when scene is generated
2. Progressive Discovery - Following clues leads to NPC introduction
3. Location Changes - NPCs present at new locations
4. Explicit Narrative Events - Someone enters the scene

**Face-to-Face Interactions:**
- Requires NPC physically present in scene

**Remote Interactions (call, phone, message):**
- Requires contact info established in memories/narrative context

**Validation Questions:**
- [ ] Was the NPC established before interaction?
- [ ] For face-to-face, is the NPC physically present?
- [ ] For remote contact, was contact info established?
- [ ] Did the NPC NOT appear from nowhere?

---

### N2. Concrete Detail Consistency
**Rule:** Specific details (vehicles, clothing, weapons, brands, physical traits) must remain consistent across all scenes.

**Detail Categories:**
- VEHICLE (car models, motorcycles)
- CLOTHING (what characters wear)
- WEAPON (specific weapons/tools)
- LOCATION (named places)
- BRAND (watches, phones, sunglasses)
- PHYSICAL_TRAIT (scars, tattoos, distinctive features)
- POSSESSION (items characters own/carry)

**Validation Questions:**
